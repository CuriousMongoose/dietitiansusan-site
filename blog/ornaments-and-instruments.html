Ornaments and Instruments

26 June 2026 · 5 min read

You stand in front of the mirror, and in about three seconds — before you have had a single conscious thought — your body has already been scored. Too much here. Not enough there. The verdict is in before the kettle has boiled. We have all been trained to ask the mirror one question: how do I look? I want to suggest that this is the wrong question.

Not because how you look doesn't matter at all — but because it is a tiny, surface question standing in the place where a far bigger one should be. It has been quietly draining the joy out of your body for years.

Two ways to see a body

Here is a distinction I have come to lean on heavily after thirteen years of clinical work.

An ornament exists to be looked at. Its entire worth is in its appearance. It sits on the shelf, it is admired or it is not, and if it chips or fades, its value drops. An ornament is judged by the eye, from the outside, and it can do nothing.

An instrument exists to be used. A violin's worth is in the music it makes. A good kitchen knife's worth is in the meals it helps you serve. You don't admire an instrument on a shelf — you pick it up, and it does something in the world. Its marks and its wear are not flaws. They are evidence that it has been living.

Diet culture has spent your whole life insisting your body is an ornament. That its job is to be looked at and scored. That a wrinkle, a scar, a softer middle after carrying children is a depreciation — a loss of value.

But Scripture never once treats your body as an ornament. Not once.

An ornament asks: do I look good enough? An instrument asks: am I available — am I ready?

What your body is actually for

Your body is the instrument through which you love the people God gave you.

These are the arms that have carried children who would not sleep. The hands that have made ten thousand meals nobody thanked you for. The legs that got you up at 3 a.m., again, the back that has bent over homework and laundry and the beds of the sick. This is not a thing to be looked at. This is a thing that has been poured out — and that is the highest compliment a body can be paid.

"So whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God."
— 1 Corinthians 10:31

Look at what that verse quietly does. It takes eating — the most ordinary, daily, bodily thing — and it points the whole of it outward and upward, to the glory of God. Not to the glory of the mirror. Not to the approval of the scale. Eating becomes fuel for a life that is for Him and for the people He's placed around your table.

And suddenly the question changes. The question is no longer how does this body look? It is what is this body for, and am I keeping it well enough to do it?

The why under everything

This is the foundation under every small practice I've been giving you.

You sit at the table and eat slowly — not to shrink the instrument, but to keep it well-tuned and well-fed for the work it does. You add the good thing to your plate — not to earn the right to be looked at, but to fuel an instrument that is being asked to carry a great deal. You pause at the fridge — not to police an ornament, but to tend the soul living inside the instrument.

When the goal is a better-looking ornament, food becomes the enemy and your body becomes a problem to be managed. It is exhausting, and it never ends, because an ornament is never quite finished.

When the goal is a well-kept instrument, food becomes provision and your body becomes a gift to be stewarded. That is a race you can actually run — and finish — because an instrument doesn't need to be flawless. It needs to be ready.

A small practice for this week

So here is the shift I'd love you to try this week. It costs nothing and changes everything.

When you pass the mirror, and the old scoring question fires — how do I look — gently answer a different one instead: what did this body let me do today?

It held a child. It walked. It worked. It made a meal. It showed up. Thank God for the instrument, out loud if you can. Thank You for this body that let me love the people You gave me today. You will be amazed how quickly gratitude crowds out the verdict, because the two cannot occupy the same three seconds.

You are not an ornament that has to be earned. You are a beloved instrument, already in use, already of immense worth in the hands that made you.
