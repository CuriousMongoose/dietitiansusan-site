Breaking the Cycle

14 April 2026 · 3 min read

Most women I've worked with don't fail at health because they lack willpower. They fail because no one ever tended the wound underneath — the one diet culture keeps mistaking for a weight problem.

Over thirteen years of working as a dietitian, I've learned a hard truth: most people don't fail because they lack self-discipline.

I've watched so many women believe that if they just had the right diet — or a little more willpower — they would finally succeed. They try something new. It works for a moment. And then life happens, and they fall back. With every fall, the feeling of failure grows a little heavier, until they feel unworthy and almost afraid to try again.

Honestly, watching that cycle breaks my heart. I hate seeing women who have so much to offer the world quietly shrink back and stop trying — all because of an emotional wound wearing the costume of a weight problem.

The wound disguised as a weight problem

That phrase is the one I keep coming back to, because it changes where we look. The number on the scale is almost never the real issue. It's the visible thing, so it's the thing we attack — with another plan, another rule, another Monday. But the ache that sends us back to the cupboard at nine at night isn't usually hunger. It's loneliness, exhaustion, old hurt, the quiet belief that we are too much and not enough at the same time.

You cannot diet your way out of a wound. You can only heal it. And you are not the problem here. The diets are.

When we get to the core of the problem, the transformation doesn't stay on the plate. It spills over into every area of life.

There is a better way

Nothing makes me feel more alive than watching a woman face her fears, find healing, and begin to believe the truth about who she was made to be. Not who the scale says she is. Not who the last failed plan said she is. Who God says she is.

That is the heart behind everything I write here: to help you heal your relationship with food and build a sustainable, healthy life rooted in truth, not trends. Not another set of rules to white-knuckle, but grace — the kind that actually fits inside a real mother's week.

If you've spent years believing the failure was yours, I'd gently offer you a different starting point. The cycle was never proof of your weakness. It was proof the method was broken. And there is a kinder, truer way to begin again — one I'll keep walking through, letter by letter, in this space.
