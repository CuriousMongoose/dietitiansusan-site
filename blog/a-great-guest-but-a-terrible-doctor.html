A Great Guest, but a Terrible Doctor

21 April 2026 · 4 min read

So much of our struggle with food isn't really about food. We try to put a snack into a God-shaped hole — cake to celebrate, ice cream to self-medicate. But food makes a wonderful guest at the table and a terrible doctor for the soul.

I once gave a talk called The Tool Chest for Our Hearts. I was speaking to a room of children and teenagers, but halfway through I realised something: we adults need these reminders just as much — if not more.

We so often try to fill a God-shaped hole in our hearts with a snack. We use cake to celebrate and ice cream to self-medicate. Food is genuinely a gift for nourishment. But God is our source of comfort, and the two were never meant to swap places.

Food makes a wonderful guest at the table, but it makes a terrible doctor for the soul. When we are hurting, we don't need a pantry. We need a Physician.

The Pause & Pray check

The next time you find yourself standing in front of the fridge when you aren't actually hungry, try this thirty-second practice. It's a way to honour the body God gave you by giving it what it actually needs — three steps I call the HALT check.

  • Breathe & believe. Three slow breaths, and a short prayer: Father, meet me here. This isn't a spiritual nicety — it quiets the fight-or-flight part of the brain enough that you can think instead of just react.
  • Check the tank. Ask honestly: am I Hungry, Angry (or anxious), Lonely, or Tired? Sometimes the answer is real hunger — and that is good news, not failure. But often the tank running low isn't the stomach.
  • Choose your tool. If you're hungry, eat — gladly, without guilt. If you're not, reach for a tool other than food.

Building a better toolbox

If you aren't physically hungry, a chocolate bar simply cannot bind up the wound of a lonely afternoon or a stressful day. So we need non-food tools in the chest — ready before the moment comes. For me, that looks like:

  • Worship — one specific song to shift the atmosphere of a room.
  • Connection — texting a friend for prayer instead of reaching for the chips.
  • Movement — a five-minute walk to clear the mental fog.
"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds."
— Psalm 147:3

A cookie is a lovely gift. It just can't heal a heart. Only He can. So this week, let's practice one small, holy reordering: going to the Creator before we go to the cupboard.

And if you've ever wondered why willpower alone never seems to hold this line — why just trying harder keeps failing you — that's exactly the question I picked up in What If It's Not About Willpower?
