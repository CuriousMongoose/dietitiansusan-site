Excellence, Not Perfection

12 June 2026 · 5 min read

Over the last few weeks I've given you three small practices. To sit — one real meal a day, at a table, with thanks. To add — one good thing to your plate before taking anything away. To pause — thirty seconds at the fridge door. So let me ask the honest question: how many days did it actually last?

If you're anything like most of the women I've sat with in my practice, the answer is something like: the first two days were beautiful, and then Wednesday happened. The lunchbox chaos. The missed call. The 9 p.m. raid you fully intended to pause for, and didn't.

And here is the thing I most want you to hear — before you do anything else with that. The slip is not the failure.

The lie that does the real damage

After thirteen years, I am convinced that the thing that keeps a woman stuck is almost never the missed meal, the second helping, or the broken streak.

It's what she says to herself afterward.

Well, that's ruined. I've blown it. I'll start again Monday. And in that one sentence — that small, familiar, devastating sentence — a single slip becomes a written-off week, and a written-off week becomes the same old cycle you have been trying to leave for years.

The cookie was never the problem. The cookie is a guest. It's the verdict you hand yourself afterward that does the damage — because that verdict is the voice of the very thing we are walking away from.

Diet culture has always run on a single engine: you are not enough yet, and one mistake proves it. It is, quite literally, the spirit of accusation dressed up as health advice. That is not the voice of the One who made you.

Excellence is the aim — not perfection

There's a distinction I teach early and often, and I want to give it to you plainly.

Perfection says: the standard is flawless, and anything less is failure. It is all-or-nothing. It cannot bend, so it breaks — and it takes your whole week down with it.

Excellence says: I am aiming faithfully in a good direction, and I expect to wobble, and the wobble is part of the walk, not the end of it. Excellence can have a bad Wednesday and still be intact on Thursday morning. Perfection cannot.

One of these is from God. The other never was.

"Being confident of this, that He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus."
— Philippians 1:6

Read who is doing the work in that verse. Not you. He began it; He will carry it on. Your slip on Wednesday does not get a vote on whether He finishes what He started. That is the most freeing sentence in nutrition I know, and it isn't even about nutrition.

Remember, too, the verb we keep returning to. In Romans 12, it is passive: not transform yourself, but be transformed. You were never the engine of this. You are the one who keeps showing back up at the table — and showing back up after a bad day is not the exception to the work. It is the work.

What grace actually does with a slip

Here is the quiet reframe I'd love you to carry.

A slip is not a verdict to be served. It is information to be received — gently, with curiosity, the way you'd treat a friend, not a suspect. That's interesting. What was actually going on for me on Wednesday? What was the tank really low on? That is not letting yourself off the hook. That is taking the hook out altogether, because the hook was never God's idea.

The woman who heals is not the one who never slips. I have never met her. The woman who heals is the one who slips, and returns — without the three days of self-punishment in between. She has simply taken the long, exhausting detour of guilt out of the loop. Slip, return. Slip, return. That short loop, repeated kindly over months, is what change actually looks like.

A small practice for this week

So this week, no new task. Just one shift.

The next time you slip — and you will, because you are a human being and not a machine — catch the sentence that comes after it. The I've blown it. And answer it out loud with something truer: No. I haven't blown anything. I'm just going to begin again at the very next meal.

Not Monday. Not next month. The next meal. Excellence doesn't wait for a fresh week — it picks up exactly where it is, mid-mess, and carries on.

You are not behind. You are not failing. You are being carried to completion by Someone who does not break His promises over a bad Wednesday.
