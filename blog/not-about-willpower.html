What If It's Not About Willpower?

28 April 2026 · 5 min read

When women hear that food is a wonderful guest but a terrible doctor, the most honest question that follows is usually this: okay — but then why can't I just stop? The answer you've been handed your whole life is wrong, and it may be the reason you've stayed stuck.

The answer most of us have been given is you need more willpower. I want to say plainly: that is not the answer. And after thirteen years in clinical practice, I've come to believe it may be the very thing keeping women trapped.

It was never a willpower problem

In thirteen years of practice, I've watched hundreds of women blame themselves for a problem willpower was never designed to solve. Here's what most people don't realise: emotional eating is not a character flaw, and it is not a discipline issue. It is a coping strategy for something underneath that hasn't been healed yet.

We think food is the problem. But food is only the visible tip of the iceberg.

What's actually under the surface

Beneath most stuck patterns with food, there is something much harder to look at:

  • Unhealed hurt — from childhood, relationships, or losses you never got to grieve.
  • Stress with no outlet, and a nervous system that has never been allowed to rest.
  • Loneliness that goes unnamed because I should be grateful.
  • Shame — often decades of it — that you've tried to outrun by being disciplined.

Food is a fast, accessible, socially acceptable way to quiet those things for twenty minutes. That is why willpower fails. You cannot will yourself out of a wound. You can only heal it.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."
— Proverbs 4:23

Solomon knew what every good counsellor knows: the heart is upstream of the behaviour. If you don't tend the heart, you'll keep treating the symptom while the source keeps bleeding.

What diet culture gets wrong

Diet culture looks at your stuck pattern and says: try harder. Faith mixed with diet culture says: you just need more discipline — pray more, be stronger. Both are the same message in different clothing: you must save yourself.

But that's not the gospel. The gospel says you already have a Saviour, and it isn't you. You don't need to white-knuckle your way to food freedom. You need to stop trying to do the one thing Christ has already done.

Be transformed

Here is the line I keep coming back to, from Romans 12:

Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.

Notice the verb. It is passive. Be transformed — not transform yourself. Your job is not to fix yourself. Your job is to show up and say yes: one meal, one honest prayer, one small addition at a time. The Spirit does the transforming. You just have to stay available.

This is slow, quiet work. Solomon's temple took seven years to build. Your relationship with food will not be rebuilt in six weeks of sheer willpower either — and that's not a failure. That's the design.

One small thing to try

The next time you notice yourself reaching for food when you're not hungry, don't white-knuckle it and don't shame yourself. Ask instead: what am I actually looking for? If it's hunger, eat. If it's hurt, sit with it for one minute before you move. That one minute — that pause — is where the Spirit meets you. Not in the dramatic overhaul. In the small, honest moment. (If you'd like the full thirty-second version of that pause, it's the Pause & Pray check.)

Perfection was never the standard. Showing up again — excellence — is. You don't need more willpower. You need to stop fighting alone.
