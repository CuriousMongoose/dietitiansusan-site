Return to the Manufacturer

30 July 2026 · 5 min read

This letter arrives on Wednesday morning, 30 July — set your table and come back then.

← All notes from the table

We're past the halfway mark of the year now. And July has a particular feeling to it, doesn't it? The January intentions are a distant memory. Winter has made everything slower, heavier, more indoors. And somewhere in the last few weeks, a quiet thought may have started circling: something feels off.

Maybe it's energy that isn't what it was. Clothes that fit differently. Evenings that keep ending at the biscuit tin. You can't always name it precisely — but you can feel it. And right behind that feeling, as reliable as clockwork, comes the pull: maybe I just need a new plan.

A new program. A stricter reset. The eating plan a friend swears by. If that pull has visited you recently, I want to offer you something better to do with it — because after thirteen years in practice, I can tell you exactly where the new plan leads, and it isn't where you're hoping.

What the pull is actually telling you

Let me say this first: the something-feels-off signal is real, and it deserves to be taken seriously. I would never talk a woman out of caring about her health.

But notice what diet culture has trained us to do with that signal. The moment something feels off, we hand the problem to a stranger. A program written for nobody in particular. A set of rules that has never met you — that doesn't know your season, your body's history, your children, your 4 p.m., your grief or your joy. We take the most personal signal a body can send, and we outsource it to the least personal solution money can buy.

Every diet quietly teaches you the same lesson: that you must be your own saviour. Follow the rules, fix yourself, earn your way back to feeling right. That job description was never yours. It's already taken.

Return to the Manufacturer

So here is the framework I keep coming back to, and it's the one I most want to put in your hands before the mid-year pull wins.

When an appliance in your kitchen starts doing something strange, you don't hand it to a stranger to guess at. You go back to the manufacturer — because the one who designed the thing knows exactly what it needs, knows what "off" means for that particular machine, and knows the difference between a fault and a feature.

You have a Manufacturer.

"For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother's womb. I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made."
— Psalm 139:13–14

Knit together. Not assembled on a production line to a generic specification — knit, deliberately, stitch by stitch, by hands that knew what they were making. The One who made your body has not lost the design. He knows what this exact season is asking of it. He knows what is actually sitting underneath the "off."

So when something feels off: Return to the Manufacturer. Before the next plan, before the search bar, before the stranger's rules — talk to Him about it. Really talk to Him about it. Not a vague "help me be healthier," but the honest, specific version: Something feels off. You made this body. Show me what it actually needs.

What He tends to say

I can't script God's side of that conversation, and I wouldn't try. But I can tell you what I've watched happen, again and again, when women pray it honestly: the answer is almost never a diet.

It is usually smaller, kinder, and far more specific. Go to bed earlier. Drink the water. Take the walk. Sit down for one real meal a day. Add the good things to your plate before you take anything away. Pause at the fridge and check what the tank is actually low on. Ask what the biscuit-tin evenings are really about. Phone the friend. Forgive the thing.

Jesus said, "do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink; or about your body… Look at the birds of the air… your heavenly Father feeds them" (Matthew 6:25–26). He isn't dismissing food or the body — He is re-ordering them. Your Father already knows what you need. The knowing was never your burden to carry alone, and it was certainly never a stranger's to sell you.

A small practice for this week

So this week, one thing. Before you search for, download, or sign up for anything — take the pull to Him first, out loud if you can:

Something feels off. You made this body. What does it actually need?

Then be quiet for a minute, and write down the first small, kind thing that comes. Do that one thing this week. Not the whole overhaul — the one thing. That is not a smaller answer than a diet. It is a much bigger one, because it comes from Someone who knows the design.

You do not have to figure your body out alone. The One who knit it together hasn't gone anywhere — and He is far easier to reach than customer service.

Blessings,
